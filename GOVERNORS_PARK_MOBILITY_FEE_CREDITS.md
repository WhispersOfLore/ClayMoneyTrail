# Governor's Park / Cathedral Oak Mobility Fee Credits, and the GBAR Ranch DRI Expansion Lead

Compiled 2026-09-23/24 from primary documents obtained via the Clay County
Clerk of Court and Comptroller's public Document Search (no login required)
and, for one item explicitly marked below, the county procurement portal via
the user's own authorized vendor account. See `research-staging/meeting-
transcripts/verification-2026-09-23/provenance.json` for hashes and full
retrieval detail (private research file; not published).

**Read this whole file before citing any figure below.** Three different
things are described here, and they must not be conflated:

1. A **verified**, primary-document-confirmed mobility-fee-credit agreement
   for Governor's Park (Section 1).
2. An **unverified lead** — a large land-use amendment discussed in two
   meeting transcripts, involving entities that are not yet fully identified
   (Section 2).
3. A **verified**, primary-document-confirmed mobility-fee-credit agreement
   for a different development, Cathedral Oak (Section 3).

## 1. Governor's Park Mobility Fee Credit Agreement — VERIFIED FACT

- **Document**: Clay County Agreement/Contract No. 2025/2026-0020, "Governors
  Park Mobility Fee Credit Agreement," executed October 28, 2025, between
  Clay County and **Governors Park Property Holdings, LLC** ("GPPH," a
  Delaware LLC, successor to D.D.I., Inc.).
- **Source**: Clay County Clerk of Court and Comptroller — Document Search,
  https://dmcc.clayclerk.com/Documents/Download/16636 (public, no login
  required). Also indexed in `data/contracts-fy25-26.json` (record
  2025/2026-0020).
- **What it does**: Replaces a 2019 predecessor agreement. GPPH is the owner
  and master developer of the Governors Park Development of Regional Impact
  (DRI), approved by BCC Ordinance No. 2009-29 (amended 2017, 2019, 2022).
  Special Condition 23 of the DRI Development Order requires GPPH to
  construct a four-lane Green Cove Springs (GCS) Bypass — a ~5-mile road from
  U.S. 17 to State Road 16 — and dedicate right-of-way for it. Special
  Condition 30 entitles GPPH to mobility-fee credit for doing so, because the
  GCS Bypass is a Designated Mobility Improvement under Clay County Mobility
  Fee Ordinance No. 2020-39.
- **The $81.1 million figure — what it is and is not**: The Mobility Fee
  Ordinance valued the GCS Bypass at $77,763,560.00 in 2020. That value
  escalates annually under an ordinance-specified construction-cost
  escalator. As of October 1, 2025 the value was $93,309,010.77, which the
  Agreement then adjusts down to **$81,138,270.20** to reflect the updated
  5-mile (vs. original 5.75-mile) alignment length. **This $81,138,270.20 is
  the maximum total mobility-fee credit GPPH can ever earn for building the
  entire bypass — it is not a cash payment from the county, and it is not a
  credit GPPH has already earned or used.** Credit is earned incrementally
  and only as construction/dedication actually happens: 10% of the value of
  each bypass segment upon the county's approval of a plat containing that
  segment's right-of-way, and the remaining 90% only upon GPPH either posting
  a completion bond/letter of credit or actually completing that segment.
  Credit earned can be used only dollar-for-dollar against GPPH's (or a
  successor's) own future Mobility Fee obligations for new construction
  within the DRI, or — subject to compliance with Florida Statute
  163.31801(10) — transferred to the adjacent Agricola Property Planned Unit
  Development (Ordinance No. 2024-14). **It is never disbursed to GPPH as
  cash.**
- **Maximum vs. earned vs. used vs. cash — apply this distinction every time
  this figure is cited**:
  - **Maximum authorized credit**: $81,138,270.20 (established by this
    document; verified fact).
  - **Credit actually earned to date**: not established here. Would require
    the plat-approval and bonding/completion records for each bypass
    segment, which have not been reviewed.
  - **Credit actually used/applied to date**: not established here. Would
    require the annual report GPPH must file with the county's Mobility Fee
    Coordinator (Section 7 of the Agreement) and/or the county's own Credit
    ledger (Section 4.b). Neither has been reviewed.
  - **Cash paid by the county to GPPH**: none — the Agreement does not
    provide for any cash payment to GPPH at all. This is a fee-offset
    mechanism, not a grant or reimbursement program.
- **Entity-identity note**: GPPH's notice address is care of a General
  Counsel in Darien, CT, with copies to **BTI Partners** (Fort Lauderdale, FL,
  Attn: Kevin Kramer) and outside counsel Morris, Manning & Martin, LLP.
  BTI Partners appears here only as a notice recipient under the Agreement —
  this document does not establish that BTI Partners owns, controls, or is
  the same entity as GPPH. Treat "BTI Partners" and "GPPH" as related but
  legally distinct until an ownership document (e.g., a Sunbiz filing or
  operating agreement) says otherwise.

## 2. GBAR Ranch Land / Governor's Park DRI Expansion — UNVERIFIED LEAD

This is a separate, much less certain finding from the same research pass.
**Do not describe this as confirmed, and do not describe it as part of the
verified $81.1 million figure above** — no primary document ties the two
together; the connection is that both concern the same DRI.

- **What the transcripts describe**: Two independent BCC/Planning Commission
  meeting transcripts (Planning Commission, July 7, 2026; BCC, July 28, 2026
  — both transmittal-hearing discussions, not final adoption) describe a
  large-scale comprehensive-plan/Future Land Use amendment, comp case
  26-00009, covering approximately 1,264–1,265 acres at the intersection of
  SR 16 West and Springbank Road, referred to in the meeting as "the GBAR
  ranch land." The change would move roughly 7 full parcels + 3 partial
  parcels from Agriculture + Commercial to Planned Community, **to be
  incorporated into the Governor's Park DRI**.
- **Named applicants**: **Governors Park Property Holdings LLC** (the same
  entity as Section 1) and **Clay Dair Property Holdings** — a name that has
  not appeared anywhere else in this project's research and has not been
  independently confirmed via Sunbiz or any Clerk record. Agents named in
  the transcript: Ellen Avery-Smith (Rogers Towers), Ray Spafford and Casey
  Dendor (England-Thims & Miller, presumed).
- **Status**: This was a transmittal hearing only (a step toward sending a
  proposed comprehensive-plan amendment to the state for review) — not a
  final adoption. The Planning Commission's July 7, 2026 vote is described in
  the transcript only as a motion to approve the transmittal; the specific
  vote count was not captured. No adoption hearing, ordinance number, or
  final BCC vote has been located for this item.
- **Evidence status**: `unverified_lead`. The transmittal vote itself is a
  specific, checkable question (an official BCC/Planning Commission agenda
  item or minutes would resolve it). Its relationship, if any, to the
  Section 1 mobility-fee-credit agreement — e.g., whether new construction
  on the expanded acreage would draw down GPPH's existing credit balance —
  is not established by anything reviewed and should be treated as an open
  question, not a connection.
- **CRITICAL NAMING-COLLISION WARNING — do not confuse these two things**:
  This project's earlier `SANDRIDGE_PROPERTY_CHAIN.md` documents a
  completely different, unrelated property called **"G Bar Ranch LLC"** /
  **"G Bar Ranch South, LLC"** (a Gustafson-family ranching entity, formed
  2012, on CR 16-A in Green Cove Springs — the parcel behind LEAD-gustafson).
  The transcript term **"GBAR ranch land"** here refers to acreage at SR 16
  West and Springbank Road, in a different part of the county, tied to
  Governor's Park/GPPH, with no relationship established to the Gustafson
  "G Bar Ranch" entity. The near-identical names are very likely a
  coincidence (or an ASR transcription rendering of a spoken nickname), but
  this has **not** been independently confirmed either way. Never merge
  these two items, and flag the name collision explicitly in any future
  writeup that touches either one.
- **Records needed to advance this lead**: official BCC/Planning Commission
  minutes or agenda packet for comp case 26-00009 (both the July 7 and July
  28, 2026 hearings, and any later adoption hearing); a Sunbiz search for
  "Clay Dair Property Holdings"; confirmation of GPPH's/Clay Dair's exact
  legal relationship to each other and to BTI Partners; and a check for
  whether "GBAR" and "G Bar Ranch" is anything more than a naming coincidence.

## 3. Cathedral Oak / Reinhold Mobility Fee Credit Agreement — VERIFIED FACT

- **Documents**: Clay County Agreement/Contract No. 2021/22-139 (original
  Mobility Fee Credit Agreement, executed March 22, 2022, between Clay
  County and Reinhold Corporation, a Florida corporation) and its First
  Amendment, 2021/2022-139 AM1 (executed May 16, 2025, recorded 2025-05-16;
  by then between the County and **Cathedral Oak, LLC**, a Florida LLC and
  assignee of Reinhold's rights as of September 20, 2023).
- **Source**: Clay County Clerk of Court and Comptroller — Document Search,
  https://dmcc.clayclerk.com/Documents/Download/16349 (public, no login
  required).
- **What it covers**: Reinhold/Cathedral Oak's ~1,789.61-acre
  silviculture/agriculture property donated right-of-way for the First
  Coast Connector road (Project No. 12), now known as Cathedral Oak Parkway,
  running from the First Coast Expressway (SR 23) easterly to U.S. 17. The
  county has already constructed a two-lane version of this road from SR 23
  to Maryland Avenue.
- **The $1,381,835.60 figure**: The First Amendment (AM1) states that
  "Cathedral Oak shall receive Credit in the amount of $1,381,835.60" for
  constructing and dedicating a specific "Additional Improvement" — the
  third and fourth (outer) lanes, associated bike lanes, and signage/striping
  on a defined 3,395-foot segment of Cathedral Oak Parkway — which the
  Amendment required Cathedral Oak to complete by August 31, 2025. This
  amount is subject to the same annual escalator as the original agreement
  (Section 3.10 of the Mobility Fee Ordinance).
- **Apply the same safeguards as Section 1**: This is an **authorized**
  credit amount tied to a specific, defined construction obligation — it is
  not, by itself, proof that the credit has been **earned** (i.e., that the
  Additional Improvement was actually completed and dedicated — the deadline
  was August 31, 2025, but completion/dedication has not been independently
  confirmed from a primary record this pass), **used** (applied against a
  Mobility Fee obligation), or **paid in cash** (this Agreement, like
  Governor's Park's, is a fee-offset mechanism with no cash-payment
  provision to Cathedral Oak).
- **Not yet reviewed**: A Second Amendment (2021/2022-139 AM2, dated
  2026-01-13) exists in the Clerk index but was not read this pass. The
  legal description (Exhibit A) of the underlying 1,789.61-acre property was
  also not read, so no parcel-level overlap between this property and any
  other Green Cove Springs-area development referenced elsewhere in this
  project's research (e.g., the Peters Creek geotechnical report site) has
  been established — entity-name proximity only.
